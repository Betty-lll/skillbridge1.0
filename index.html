What makes it different

Theory teaches what.
SkillBridge teaches what happens next.

Three design choices separate SkillBridge from static case studies and traditional virtual internships.

01 · Dynamic scenarios

The project responds to what you actually do.

Most virtual internships are linear. Read, complete, submit, done. SkillBridge isn't. Every decision a student makes forks the storyline — changing client mood, project scope, team dynamics, and final outcomes.

  • Branching narratives with hundreds of decision states per scenario
  • AI-generated stakeholder responses tailored to student reasoning
  • Replayable — no two attempts unfold the same way

02 · AI stakeholders

Three perspectives. In real time.

A real workplace isn't one-way. Students don't just receive feedback — they negotiate with a demanding manager, manage a shifting client, and respond to a peer who disagrees. All three respond to tone, reasoning, and timing.

  • Distinct AI personalities trained on professional communication patterns
  • Stakeholders remember context across the entire project
  • Communication scored as carefully as the decisions themselves

03 · Consequence learning

Every choice shows up later.

Poor communication leads to unclear deliverables. Ignoring a colleague's input affects team morale. Even a strong decision loses value if a student can't explain it. SkillBridge tracks all of it — and surfaces it in the final report.

  • Cause-and-effect chains tracked across every day of the simulation
  • Stakeholder relationships build or erode based on student behaviour
  • Final report shows how decisions compounded — not just what was completed

Trust & safety

An AI product, designed responsibly.

We've built SkillBridge to a higher bar than the typical edtech tool — because we're working with student data, AI judgement, and outcomes that matter.

P

Privacy first

We collect the minimum data needed to deliver the service. Students can review, export, or delete their data at any time. A full Privacy Impact Assessment is published on request.

B

Bias auditing

Skill scoring is audited quarterly for fairness across English-as-a-second-language students, communication styles, and cultural backgrounds. Audit reports are shared with partner universities.

F

Developmental, not deterministic

All AI feedback is labelled as developmental — designed to support student growth, never to substitute for hiring decisions or formal academic assessment.

E

Educator oversight

Career advisors and faculty have visibility into how students are using the platform — including the ability to flag, review, or contextualise any AI-generated feedback.

Common questions

Things everyone asks us.

How is SkillBridge different from Forage?+
Forage offers static, linear virtual experiences — students read a brief and submit preset tasks. SkillBridge is dynamic and consequential: AI stakeholders push back on student reasoning, scenarios branch based on choices, and the final report reflects how students decided and communicated, not just what they completed.
Is the AI biased toward certain communication styles?+
We audit our scoring models quarterly for fairness across English-as-a-second-language students, neurodivergent communication patterns, and cultural communication styles. Audit results are published to partner universities, and our skill model explicitly avoids penalising non-native phrasing.
What student data do you collect?+
We collect the minimum needed: name, email, university, scenario responses, and skill scores. All data is stored on Australian servers, retained only for the duration of the license agreement, and never sold or shared with recruiters without explicit, separate consent. Our full Privacy Impact Assessment is available on request.
Can students use SkillBridge for free?+
Yes — students never pay. SkillBridge is sold as an institutional license to universities, who then provide free access to their students. We also run a free open library of three introductory scenarios for any student to try independently.
Can scenarios be customised for specific programs?+
Institutional license customers can co-develop custom scenarios with our content team. We've built bespoke modules for healthcare programs (clinical handovers), law schools (client intake), and engineering faculties (stakeholder briefing) in our pilot cohort.
How long does each simulation take?+
Most simulations run 60–90 minutes, designed to fit a single sitting. Students can pause and return at any time. Some advanced multi-day scenarios run 3–5 hours across multiple sessions.
Do employers see student scores?+
Not by default. Skill reports belong to the student. We've designed an opt-in feature where students can share specific reports with employers as part of an application, but this is always student-initiated and clearly labelled as developmental data, not a recruitment screening score.
